# Sources

The original request was a link list of shortened LinkedIn URLs (`lnkd.in/…`), which are not resolvable. These are the canonical destinations behind that curation, verified and annotated.

## Standards

- [Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities (W3C COGA)](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) — the reference document. Dense, and ironically hard to read for its own audience. Source of the 8 objectives.
- [Cognitive Accessibility at W3C (WAI)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/cognitive/) — more digestible entry point.
- [Supplemental Guidance to WCAG 2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/#-cognitive-accessibility-guidance)
- [Cognitive accessibility — MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Cognitive_accessibility) — friendliest introduction.
- [WebAIM: Evaluating Cognitive Web Accessibility](https://webaim.org/articles/evaluatingcognitive/) — practical evaluation checklist.

## The GOV.UK poster series (the backbone of most of the Do/Don't tables)

- [Dos and don'ts on designing for accessibility](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2016/09/02/dos-and-donts-on-designing-for-accessibility/) — Karwai Pun, Home Office. Covers autism, D/deaf, dyslexia, motor, low vision, screen readers.
- [UKHomeOffice/posters on GitHub](https://github.com/UKHomeOffice/posters) — all posters, including the anxiety one. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- [HTML versions](https://ukhomeoffice.github.io/accessibility-posters/)
- [Designing for people with dyscalculia and low numeracy](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2022/11/28/designing-for-people-with-dyscalculia-and-low-numeracy/) — McFadyen, Malic, Parker (DWP/HMRC).
- [The dyscalculia poster with the research behind each tip](https://github.com/abbott567/dyscalculia-poster#readme) — Craig Abbott. The reasoning, not just the rule.
- [DWP Accessibility Manual](https://accessibility-manual.dwp.gov.uk/tools-and-resources/accessibility-posters)
- [Designing accessible services: don't exclude the neurodiverse](https://dwpdigital.blog.gov.uk/2022/06/30/designing-accessible-services-dont-exclude-the-neurodiverse/)

## Curated collections

- [Neurodiversity and UX: Essential Resources for Cognitive Accessibility](https://stephaniewalter.design/blog/neurodiversity-and-ux-essential-resources-for-cognitive-accessibility/) — Stéphanie Walter. Kept current; the best single reading list.
- [Designing For Neurodiversity](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/06/designing-for-neurodiversity/) — Vitaly Friedman, Smashing Magazine. The article behind the LinkedIn list.
- [Neurodiversity Design System](https://neurodiversity.design/) — Will Soward. Design standards and principles for accessible learning interfaces; much of it generalises.

## Prevalence figures used in this skill

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Autism ~1 in 31 US 8-year-olds (2022 data, published April 2025) | [CDC ADDM](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/data-research/index.html) |
| Dyscalculia ~1 in 20 in the UK; dyslexia ~1 in 10 to 20 | [GOV.UK Design Notes](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2022/11/28/designing-for-people-with-dyscalculia-and-low-numeracy/) |
| Low numeracy affects ~half of UK working-age adults (~17m) | [National Numeracy / Skills for Life Survey](https://www.nationalnumeracy.org.uk/research-and-resources/skills-life-survey-2011) |
| Red–green colour blindness ~8% of men, ~0.5% of women (Northern European ancestry) | Colour Blind Awareness / standard ophthalmology figures |

Treat all of these as estimates. Diagnostic criteria, awareness, and access to diagnosis vary enormously by country and by demographic — a low prevalence figure often measures diagnosis, not people.
