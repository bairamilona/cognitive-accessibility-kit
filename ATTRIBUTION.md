# Attribution

This kit is a synthesis. Almost none of the underlying knowledge is original to it — the value is in the collection, the structure, and the operational detail. Credit belongs to the people below.

## Standards and specifications

- **W3C Web Accessibility Initiative** — [Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/), source of the eight objectives, and the WCAG 2.2 success criteria referenced throughout. W3C documents are used under the [W3C Document Licence](https://www.w3.org/copyright/document-license/); the objective titles are quoted, not reproduced wholesale.

## Curation and articles drawn on

- **Stéphanie Walter** — [Neurodiversity and UX: Essential Resources for Cognitive Accessibility](https://stephaniewalter.design/blog/neurodiversity-and-ux-essential-resources-for-cognitive-accessibility/). The best-maintained reading list in this field, and the route to a large share of the sources here.
- **Vitaly Friedman** — [Designing For Neurodiversity](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/06/designing-for-neurodiversity/), Smashing Magazine, and the accompanying per-topic summaries. The structure of this kit follows the topic breakdown from that collection.
- **Will Soward** — [Neurodiversity Design System](https://neurodiversity.design/).
- **Gareth Ford Williams** — [BBC Sensory Environment Checklist](https://bbc.github.io/uxd-cognitive/) and writing on accessible typefaces.
- **Irina Rusakova**, **Eva Katharina Wolf**, **Stephen Belyea**, **Kate Hughes**, **Demelza Feltham (Tetralogical)**, **Hera Hussain (Chayn)**, **Content Design London** — per-condition guidance, cited individually in [`sources.md`](neurodiversity-design/references/sources.md).

## Prevalence data

- Autism prevalence: [CDC ADDM Network](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/data-research/index.html)
- Dyscalculia, dyslexia, low numeracy: [GOV.UK Design Notes](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2022/11/28/designing-for-people-with-dyscalculia-and-low-numeracy/) and [National Numeracy](https://www.nationalnumeracy.org.uk/)
- Neurodivergent trait estimates (15–40%): as cited by Vitaly Friedman, Smashing Magazine

All prevalence figures are estimates. Diagnostic criteria, awareness, and access to diagnosis vary enormously by country and demographic — a low figure often measures diagnosis rates, not people.
